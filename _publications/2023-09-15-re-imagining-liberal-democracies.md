## Abstract

We will remember the COVID-19 pandemic as one of the most impactful events of the 21st century. The pandemic has forced us to take several steps back and refocus our views on society, economics and politics. The efficiency of proper policy implementation depends on widespread public adherence to measures put forward by the government. This further requires a high level of trust in the political structure. Using the Caribbean region as our case, we examine how the indicators of liberal democracy can be undermined by emergency measures implemented during the pandemic. We also evaluate the shift, if any, back to liberal democratic policies in these countries as the pandemic seemingly comes to its end. We raise and defend an argument that the COVID-19 pandemic has had a nuanced impact on liberal democracies. In some instances, it militates against liberal democratic principles. In another sense, it could create a paradigm shift and new opportunities for how we view liberal democratic values. We conclude – against the tide – that liberal democracies may not be ideal in pandemic times. Instead, crises such as pandemics require a more collectivist approach to government.
